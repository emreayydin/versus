The core difference is simple: a stock makes you an owner, a bond makes you a lender.
Buying a stock means owning a small slice of a company and sharing in its future.
That ownership means your returns can climb or fall with how the business performs.
Buying a bond means lending your money to a government or a company instead.
In return, you are promised fixed interest payments on a set schedule.
Stock owners hope for growth, while bond lenders expect steady predictability.
One path shares in both the upside and the downside of a business.
The other simply follows a fixed timetable of payments until it ends.
