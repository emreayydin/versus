The key difference under time is that stocks can be held indefinitely, while bonds always come with a fixed maturity date.
A share of stock may be held for one year, for ten years, or even for an entire lifetime, since there is no deadline attached to it at all.
There is no moment when a company is required to buy back its own stock and return the invested money to its shareholders.
A bond, however, functions as a loan that includes a clear finish line, known as the maturity date, written directly into its terms.
When that date finally arrives, the borrower repays the original amount in full, and the bond itself then simply comes to an end.
Bonds can mature in one year, in ten years, or even in thirty years, yet once it is set, that maturity date never changes.
Stocks, on the other hand, keep existing indefinitely until someone decides to sell them or until the company itself ceases to exist.
