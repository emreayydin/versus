Risk sits differently for each one, since stockholders can lose value in hard times while bondholders usually stand closer to the front of the line.
If a company struggles, its stock price can fall sharply, sometimes losing significant value, and shareholders absorb that drop.
This kind of risk is often called ownership risk, since it depends directly on how well or poorly the underlying business performs over time.
Bondholders are usually promised fixed interest payments and full repayment of their loan, ahead of any payout to stockholders.
That relative safety only holds as long as the company remains financially strong enough to meet its promised payments.
If a company cannot meet its obligations, it can default on its bonds, leaving bondholders facing real financial losses as well.
In a bankruptcy, bondholders are typically paid before stockholders, though stockholders may end up receiving little or nothing at all.
