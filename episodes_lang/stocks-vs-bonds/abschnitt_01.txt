A stock is ownership in a company, while a bond is a loan you make to one, expecting your money back with interest over time.
When you buy a stock, you become a part owner of that business, sharing directly in its future profits, its losses, and its risks.
Even holding just one single share means you technically own a tiny stake in the company's assets, its debts, and its future earnings.
A bond, on the other hand, means you are lending your own money to a company or a government, usually for a fixed period of time.
In return for that loan, the borrower promises to repay your money in full, along with regular interest payments over an agreed period of time.
As a stock owner, your money can grow or shrink together with how well the company actually performs in the wider market over time.
As a bond holder, you typically receive a fixed, steady payment on a set schedule, no matter how the company's business is going.
