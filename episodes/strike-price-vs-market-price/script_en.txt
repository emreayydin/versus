An option contract fixes one price for the future, while the real price keeps moving.
The gap between them is where an option's value comes from.
The strike price is the fixed price at which the option lets you buy or sell the stock.
It is set when the contract is created and does not change.
The market price is what the stock trades for right now.
A right to buy at fifty is worth more when the market price is seventy.
The difference: the strike is the agreed price, the market price is the live price.
Option buyers pay a premium for that right, which they can lose entirely.
It matters because the option only makes sense relative to where the market is.
The strike is the line in the sand. The market price is the tide moving around it.
