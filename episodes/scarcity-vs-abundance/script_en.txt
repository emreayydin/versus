Why does a diamond cost more than water, even though water keeps you alive?
The answer sits in two words: scarcity and abundance.
Diamonds are rare, hard to mine, and supply barely grows each year.
Because so few exist, buyers compete, and the price climbs higher.
Water covers most of the planet, and pipes deliver it right to your home.
Because it's everywhere, the price per liter stays close to nothing.
The real difference is not how useful something is, it's how scarce it is compared to demand.
This same rule decides why gold holds value while a currency can lose it through printing.
Usefulness keeps you alive, but scarcity relative to demand sets the price.
