The Fed moves one rate, and your card interest follows. Here is the chain.
Two rates sit at the start of it.
The federal funds rate is what banks charge each other for overnight loans.
The Federal Reserve sets a target range for it.
The prime rate is what banks charge their most reliable business customers.
By convention, it is usually about three percentage points above the federal funds rate.
The difference: the federal funds rate is between banks, the prime rate is a base for loans to customers.
Many credit cards and home equity lines are priced as prime plus a margin.
It matters because a Fed move usually reaches variable-rate loans quickly.
Fed funds is the wholesale price. Prime is the retail starting point.
