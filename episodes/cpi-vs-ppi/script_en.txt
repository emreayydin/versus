Two inflation numbers show up every month, and they rarely agree.
One tracks what you pay, the other tracks what producers earn.
The Consumer Price Index measures prices for things you actually buy.
That means groceries, rent, gasoline, and everyday services.
The Producer Price Index measures prices at the factory or farm gate.
It shows what businesses receive before goods ever reach a shelf.
The difference is whose price is being measured, yours or theirs.
This matters because producer prices often move before consumer prices do.
So when PPI rises, CPI sometimes follows a few months later.
Together they trace inflation from the production line to your wallet.
