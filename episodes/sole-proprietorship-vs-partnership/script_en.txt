One owner keeps all the profit. Two owners share the profit and the risk.
The line between them sits in paperwork, liability, and control.
A sole proprietorship is just one person running a business under their own name.
There's no legal wall between the owner and the business, so debts become personal too.
Setting one up often takes nothing more than simply opening for business.
A partnership means two or more people run the business together.
Each partner usually shares both the profits and any debts the business owes.
Decisions and income get split according to whatever agreement they sign.
The difference is who stands alone and who shares the risk when debts come due.
That split matters most when a creditor comes looking for someone to pay.
Neither structure is safer by default, it depends on how the risk gets divided.
