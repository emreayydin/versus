One is a number. The other is a whole arrangement built around that number.
Both show up on the same statement, so people treat the two words as one thing.
A credit limit is the highest balance a single card or account is allowed to carry.
Once you reach that number, the account simply stops approving new charges.
A credit line is the underlying facility, the deal that lets you borrow, repay, and borrow again.
That facility can sit behind one account or several, and its size becomes the limit you see.
The difference is that a credit limit is the ceiling, and a credit line is the pipe beneath it.
This shows up with things like a home equity line, where one line can feed several linked accounts.
Knowing which one you're looking at makes the number on your statement easier to read.
