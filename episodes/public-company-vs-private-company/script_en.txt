One has to show you its numbers. The other never has to.
The line between them decides who gets to see inside a business.
A public company sells shares on a stock exchange, open to anyone.
It must publish earnings and risks in public reports every quarter.
Anyone can buy a piece of it in seconds.
A private company stays in the hands of its founders or a few investors.
It shares its numbers only with those investors, not the public.
Buying in usually means finding an owner willing to sell directly.
The difference is not size, it is how much the company has to reveal.
That openness protects buyers of stock but can slow down decisions.
Public means everyone can look in, private means only a few can.
