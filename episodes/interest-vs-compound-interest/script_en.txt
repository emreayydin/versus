One kind of interest pays only on your deposit. The other pays on itself too.
Here's how that one rule ends up worth very different amounts of money.
Simple interest is calculated only on the original amount you put in.
Put in a thousand dollars at five percent, and you earn fifty dollars every year.
Compound interest is calculated on the original amount plus all interest already earned.
After twenty years, that thousand dollars grows very differently under each rule.
The real difference is compound interest pays you interest on interest, not just on your money.
This gap matters most over long stretches of time, like decades of saving.
So one grows in a straight line, and the other grows like a snowball.
