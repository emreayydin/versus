When you click buy, someone has to sell. Usually it is not another regular investor.
Two players make the trade happen.
A broker is the firm that takes your order and sends it to the market for you.
It acts on your behalf and may charge a fee or earn money in other ways.
A market maker stands ready to buy and sell a stock all day, quoting both prices.
It earns small amounts from the gap between the buying and selling price.
The difference: the broker represents you, the market maker trades with you.
Some brokers are paid by market makers for sending them orders, which is called payment for order flow.
It matters because who executes your order can affect the price you get.
The broker is your messenger. The market maker is the shop on the other side.
