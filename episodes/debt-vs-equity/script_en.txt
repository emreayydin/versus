One promises you get paid back. The other promises nothing at all.
Both are ways a company gets money to grow, but they work very differently.
Debt means someone lends the company money, expecting it back with interest.
If the company fails, lenders get paid before anyone else.
The company owes that money no matter how well or badly things go.
Equity means someone buys a piece of the company itself.
An investor becomes part owner, sharing both the profits and the losses.
If the company fails, shareholders are paid last, if anything is left.
The difference is who carries the risk when things go wrong.
This is why lenders often get lower but steadier returns than shareholders.
One is a loan with a fixed promise, the other is ownership with no guarantee.
