Two numbers claim to explain why your fund beat the market. Only one really does.
By the end you will see why one number rewards skill, and the other only measures risk.
Alpha is the extra return a manager adds beyond what the market alone explains.
The market rises ten percent this year, the fund rises fourteen percent instead.
That extra four percent belongs to alpha, credited to the manager's decisions.
Beta measures something different: how much a fund moves compared to the market.
A beta of one point two means the fund swings twenty percent more, both up and down.
If the market falls ten percent, that fund is expected to fall twelve percent.
The difference is that beta shows how much risk you carried, alpha shows what you gained beyond it.
This split matters most when funds that beat the market are compared to each other.
Beta explains the ride you took, alpha explains what the driver actually added.
