A bond says five percent on the label. You might still earn more, or less.
That is because a bond has two different rates.
The coupon rate is the fixed interest the bond pays on its face value, usually a thousand dollars.
A five percent coupon pays fifty dollars a year, no matter what the bond costs later.
Yield to maturity is the yearly return you get if you buy at today's price and hold until the end.
Buy that bond for nine hundred dollars, and you also gain a hundred at the end, so your yield is above five.
The difference: the coupon rate is fixed by the bond, the yield to maturity depends on the price you pay.
When market rates rise, bond prices fall, and yields climb above the coupon.
It matters when comparing bonds, because the coupon alone hides what you actually earn.
The coupon is what the bond pays. The yield is what you earn at the price you paid.
