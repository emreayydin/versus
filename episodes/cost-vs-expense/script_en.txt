You use these two words like they're one. Bookkeepers keep them apart.
The line between them shapes what your profit looks like on paper.
A cost is money you pay to get something of value.
Buying a machine for ten thousand dollars is a cost.
That cost sits on the books as an asset, until it gets used up.
An expense is the part of that value used up in a given period.
Each year, a slice of the machine's cost becomes an expense.
That yearly slice lowers the profit shown on the income statement.
The difference: cost is what you paid, expense is what got used up.
This split matters most when a purchase is meant to last for years.
Cost tells you what you own. Expense tells you what you spent to run the year.
