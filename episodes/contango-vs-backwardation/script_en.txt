Oil for next year can cost more than oil today. Sometimes it costs less.
Traders have two odd words for those two situations.
Contango means the futures price is higher than the spot price you pay today.
If oil is seventy dollars now and a one year contract is seventy four, that is contango.
It often reflects the cost of storing, insuring and financing the goods until later.
Backwardation is the reverse: the future price is below today's price.
It often shows up when supply is tight and buyers want the goods right now.
The difference: in contango later costs more, in backwardation now costs more.
Funds that keep rolling futures can lose money in contango even when the spot price stays flat.
Contango prices in the cost of waiting. Backwardation prices in the need for now.
