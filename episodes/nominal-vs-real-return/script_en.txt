One number tells you what you earned. The other tells you what it's worth.
Both come from the same investment, but they answer different questions.
Nominal return is the percentage gain your account shows, before anything else.
If you put in a hundred dollars and end up with a hundred and five, that's five percent nominal.
Real return takes that same gain and subtracts what inflation ate away.
If prices rose three percent that year, your real return is closer to two percent.
The real difference is that nominal return ignores prices, real return doesn't.
This gap matters most over long periods, when small differences in inflation compound.
Knowing which number you're looking at tells you what your money can actually buy.
