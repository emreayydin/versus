Two pills, same active ingredient, very different price. Most of the gap is the name.
This applies to medicine and many supermarket shelves.
Brand name products carry a well-known label and the cost of building and marketing it.
Generic medicines must contain the same active ingredient in the same dose.
In the US, the FDA requires generics to work the same way in the body.
That is why a generic can cost a fraction of the brand.
The difference: the brand sells the name and its marketing, the generic sells the same ingredient without it.
In food, generic store brands are often made in similar factories, but recipes can differ.
It matters for regular purchases, where small savings repeat every week.
A brand is an ingredient plus a story. A generic is mostly just the ingredient.
