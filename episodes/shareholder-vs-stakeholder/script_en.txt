One owns the company. The other just lives with its decisions.
Companies talk about both, but they answer to only one of them.
A shareholder holds a piece of the company, usually as stock.
That share gives voting rights and a claim on future profits.
If the company does well, the value of that share can rise.
A stakeholder is anyone affected by what the company does.
Employees, customers, neighbors, even the local river count.
None of them own a piece, but decisions still change their lives.
The difference is ownership: shareholders own it, stakeholders live with it.
That gap shapes who a company's board actually feels responsible to.
Both words describe a relationship to a company, just not the same one.
