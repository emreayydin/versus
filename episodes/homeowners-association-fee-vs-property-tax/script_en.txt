Even after the mortgage, homes have monthly costs. Two go very different places.
One goes to your neighbors, the other to the government.
An HOA fee is paid to a homeowners association in many condos and planned communities.
It covers shared things like landscaping, pools, roofs or security.
Property tax is paid to local government, based on the assessed value of your home.
It funds schools, roads, police and fire services.
The difference: HOA fees pay for your community's shared costs, property tax pays for public services.
Both can rise over time, and unpaid HOA fees can even lead to a lien on the home.
It matters when comparing homes, because two similar prices can hide very different monthly costs.
The HOA fee runs the neighborhood. Property tax runs the town.
