Signing for someone else's loan sounds kind. How much risk you take depends on one word.
Co-signer and guarantor are not always the same promise.
A co-signer signs the loan alongside the borrower and is usually responsible from day one.
The lender can ask the co-signer for payment just as easily as the borrower.
A guarantor promises to pay only if the borrower fails to pay.
The lender usually has to go to the borrower first before turning to the guarantor.
The difference: a co-signer shares the debt from the start, a guarantor steps in only as a backup.
The exact rules depend on the contract and the country, so the wording of the document decides.
It matters for your own credit, because a missed payment can show up on your report too.
A co-signer is a second borrower. A guarantor is a safety net behind the first one.
