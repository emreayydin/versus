Paying your card on the due date is not always the same as paying no interest.
The gap between those two ideas has a name.
The due date is the deadline for at least the minimum payment on your credit card statement.
Miss it, and you can face a late fee and a mark on your credit report.
The grace period is the time between the end of your statement and that due date.
In the US, card issuers must give you at least twenty one days for it.
Pay the full balance within it, and new purchases usually cost no interest at all.
The difference: the due date avoids late fees, the grace period with a full payment avoids interest.
It matters if you pay only the minimum, because interest then usually starts building on the rest.
The due date is the deadline. The grace period is the interest free window before it.
