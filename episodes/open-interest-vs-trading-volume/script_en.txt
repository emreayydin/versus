A futures market can trade a million contracts in a day and still shrink.
Two numbers on every options and futures screen explain how.
Trading volume counts how many contracts changed hands during the day.
Open interest counts how many contracts exist and have not been closed yet.
If two traders open a brand new contract, volume rises by one and open interest by one.
If one of them later passes it to a third trader, volume rises again but open interest stays the same.
If both sides close their positions, open interest falls by one.
The difference: volume measures activity today, open interest measures positions still in play.
Rising volume with falling open interest often means traders are closing out, not opening.
Volume is how busy the market was. Open interest is how much is still at stake.
