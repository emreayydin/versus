Both are money you can only spend in one store. The rules behind them are different.
And that can decide whether the balance survives.
A gift card is bought with real money, usually as a present for someone else.
In the US, federal rules say most gift cards cannot expire within five years.
Store credit is usually given by the store itself, often when you return something without a receipt.
Its rules depend on the store and the state, and some do expire.
The difference: a gift card is something someone paid for, store credit replaces a refund.
Either way, if the store closes for good, the balance can be lost.
A gift card is money in disguise. Store credit is a refund you can only spend there.
