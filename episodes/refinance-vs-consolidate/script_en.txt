Both promise a better loan. One changes a loan, the other combines several.
They overlap, but they solve different problems.
To refinance means replacing an existing loan with a new one on different terms.
Someone with a mortgage at seven percent might refinance into one at six.
To consolidate means rolling several debts into a single loan with one payment.
Three credit cards and a store loan become one monthly bill.
The difference: refinancing changes the terms of a debt, consolidating changes how many debts you have.
A consolidation loan is often also a refinance, if the new rate is lower.
Either way, a longer term can mean more interest overall, even with a lower monthly payment.
Refinance swaps a loan. Consolidate merges loans. Sometimes one step does both.
