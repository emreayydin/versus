One founder owns everything and grows slow. One owns less and grows fast.
Both pay for a company's growth, just with a different kind of currency.
Bootstrapping means the company grows only from its own revenue and savings.
There is no investor to please, but also no cash to speed things up.
Every dollar spent has to be earned first, so growth stays tied to sales.
Funding means outside investors put money in now, for a share of the company later.
That cash can hire staff and buy ads long before the company earns it back.
In exchange, the founder gives up part ownership and often part control.
The difference is who takes the risk and who owns the upside.
This matters most when a market moves fast and being first pays off.
Bootstrapping keeps control, funding buys speed, and both can build a company.
