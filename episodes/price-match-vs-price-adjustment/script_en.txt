You bought a TV, and a week later it is cheaper. Some stores will give you money back.
Two store policies cover two different situations.
A price match means a store lowers its price to match a competitor's advertised price.
It usually happens before or at the moment you pay.
A price adjustment refunds the difference if the same store lowers its own price after you bought.
It often only applies within a short window, like fourteen days.
The difference: a price match is about another store, an adjustment is about the same store later.
Many stores exclude sales events, marketplaces or clearance items.
It matters for big purchases, where keeping the receipt can pay off.
A match beats the competitor. An adjustment fixes the timing.
