Two people click buy on the same share. One gets it now, the other gets their price.
Which one you get depends on the order type.
A market order says: buy or sell right away at the best price available.
It almost always goes through, but in a fast market the price can be a bit worse than expected.
A limit order says: only at this price or better.
Set a limit of fifty dollars, and you will never pay fifty one for that share.
But if the price never reaches fifty, the order may simply never be filled.
The difference: a market order guarantees the trade, a limit order guarantees the price.
It matters most for shares that trade rarely, where prices can jump between two trades.
Market order means certainty about speed. Limit order means certainty about price.
