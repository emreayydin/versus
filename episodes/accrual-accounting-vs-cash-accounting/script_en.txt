How can a company report a profit while its bank account sits at zero?
The answer lies in when money gets counted, not when it moves.
Cash accounting counts money only when it actually lands in the account.
Sell something today, get paid in sixty days, and it only counts in two months.
That makes cash accounting simple, but slow to show what's really happening.
Accrual accounting counts money the moment a deal is made, not when it's paid.
Sell today, get paid in sixty days, and the sale still counts today.
That gives a fuller picture, but it can hide when the cash is actually short.
The difference is timing: cash counts money when it moves, accrual counts it when it's earned.
Investors read accrual profit, but check cash flow to see if the cash is really there.
Both describe the same business, just from two different clocks.
