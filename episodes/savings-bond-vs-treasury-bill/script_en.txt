Both are loans to the US government. One is built for months, the other for years.
They work in very different ways.
A Treasury bill is a short loan, maturing in a few weeks up to one year.
It is sold below its face value, and the gap at maturity is your interest.
A savings bond, like the Series I bond, is meant for households and longer periods.
I bonds must be held at least one year, and selling before five years costs three months of interest.
The difference: T-bills are short and tradable, savings bonds are longer and tied to the owner.
Purchase limits apply to savings bonds, but not in the same way to bills.
It matters for when you might need the money back.
A T-bill is a short parking spot. A savings bond is a longer garage.
