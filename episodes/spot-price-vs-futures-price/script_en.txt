Why do two prices exist for the exact same barrel of oil?
One is set right now. The other is a promise about a future date.
The spot price is what someone pays to buy something today, right now.
You hand over money, you get the goods, and the deal is done.
The futures price is agreed today, for delivery weeks or months ahead.
Both sides lock in that number now, even though nothing changes hands yet.
The difference is timing: spot is a price for now, futures is a price for later.
Farmers, airlines and traders use futures to know their costs before harvest or flight.
Same product, two prices - one for today, one for a promise about tomorrow.
