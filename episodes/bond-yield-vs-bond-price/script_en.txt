One number rises while the other falls, on the exact same bond.
The reason comes down to how price and yield are linked together.
A bond's price is what someone actually pays to buy it on the market.
That price can trade above or below the bond's original face value.
It moves up or down as investors decide the bond is worth more or less.
A bond's yield is the actual return you earn each year, in percent.
It comes from dividing the interest payment by the price you paid.
So when the price falls, the same payment turns into a higher yield.
The difference is that price is what you pay, and yield is what you earn from it.
This shows up whenever interest rates change, because it pushes prices the opposite way.
Same bond, two numbers, always moving in opposite directions.
