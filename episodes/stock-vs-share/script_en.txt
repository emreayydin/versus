One word names a single piece. The other can mean the whole pile.
The difference sounds small, but it shapes how you read a portfolio.
A share is one unit of ownership in a specific company.
If a company has one million shares, owning one gives you a tiny slice.
Stock is the broader word, it can mean all the shares of one company combined.
It also means the whole asset class, as in stocks versus bonds.
The difference: a share is the piece, stock is the category it belongs to.
This matters when someone says they bought stock but means one single share.
Same money, same ownership, just two ways of naming how much of it you count.
