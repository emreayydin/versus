One side lets you walk away. The other side means you have to follow through.
Both let two people agree today on a price for tomorrow, but the deal itself works differently.
An option gives the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell later.
If the price moves against them, they can simply let the option expire and lose only the fee they paid.
That fee is called the premium, and it's the most someone can lose.
A future is a binding contract, both sides commit to trading at a set price on a set date.
If the price moves against you, you still have to buy or sell as agreed.
There is no walking away, and the losses can be far larger than any fee.
The difference is options buy you a choice, futures lock you into a commitment.
This matters most when markets move fast, since one side's loss is capped and the other's isn't.
Both let you agree on tomorrow's price today, but only one asks you to see it through.
