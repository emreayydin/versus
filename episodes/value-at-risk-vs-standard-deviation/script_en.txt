Two risk numbers for the same portfolio. One talks about normal days, one about bad ones.
Banks use both, and they answer different questions.
Standard deviation measures how far returns usually spread around their average, up and down.
A high number just means the value swings a lot, in both directions.
Value at risk gives one loss figure for a given chance and time period.
A one day value at risk of ten thousand at ninety five percent: on most days, losses stay below that.
The difference: standard deviation describes typical swings, value at risk puts a number on a bad day.
But value at risk says nothing about how bad the other five days can get.
That blind spot was widely criticized after the two thousand eight crisis.
Standard deviation shows how bumpy it is. Value at risk shows where the bad days start.
