One number grows because the economy did. The other grows because prices did.
Both are called GDP growth, and the gap between them can be huge.
Nominal GDP adds up everything produced, valued at today's prices.
If prices rise five percent and output stays flat, nominal GDP still climbs five percent.
Real GDP takes the same output but values it at prices from a fixed base year.
That strips out inflation, so it only rises when the economy actually makes more.
The difference is what moves the number: real output or rising prices.
Economists watch real GDP to see actual growth, not just prices catching up.
Nominal tells you the size of the number. Real tells you the size of the economy.
