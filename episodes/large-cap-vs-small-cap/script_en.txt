A giant and a tiny firm can both be listed. Size changes everything.
Investors sort companies by market value.
Large cap usually means a market value above ten billion dollars.
These companies tend to be established, with steady sales and many investors watching them.
Small cap usually means roughly two hundred fifty million to two billion dollars.
They can grow faster, but their prices often swing more and they fail more often.
The difference: large caps trade more stability, small caps trade more growth potential and more risk.
The exact size limits vary between index providers.
It matters when building a mix, because the two often behave differently.
Large cap is the giant. Small cap is the fast but fragile climber.
