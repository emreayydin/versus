Two stocks cost twenty times their profit. One may still be the cheaper one.
A second number explains why.
The PE ratio is the share price divided by the profit per share over a year.
A PE of twenty means you pay twenty dollars for every dollar of yearly profit.
The PEG ratio divides that PE by how fast profits are expected to grow.
PE twenty with twenty percent growth gives a PEG of one. With five percent growth, it is four.
The difference: the PE ratio looks at profit today, the PEG ratio adds expected growth.
But growth is only a forecast, so the PEG can be wrong when the forecast is.
It matters when comparing companies that grow at very different speeds.
PE asks how expensive it is now. PEG asks how expensive it is for the growth you get.
