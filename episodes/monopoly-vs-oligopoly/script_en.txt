One firm sets the price alone. A few firms just don't compete on it.
Both shapes look different from the outside, but the effect can feel the same.
A monopoly is one company with no real competitor in its market.
Without rivals, it can set higher prices without losing customers.
Buyers have nowhere else to go, so the company holds the power.
An oligopoly is a market with only a few large companies.
They compete on paper, but often move prices in the same direction.
No single firm has to control it all, watching each other is enough.
The difference is one voice deciding versus a few voices agreeing.
This matters in markets like airlines, phone plans, or utilities.
Fewer sellers rarely means more choice for the person paying the bill.
