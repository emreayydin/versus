One tells you what should happen. The other tells you what will happen.
Businesses use both, but for very different reasons.
A budget is a plan, fixed at the start of the year for spending and income.
It sets targets: how much to spend on marketing, how much revenue to expect.
Once set, a budget usually stays the same, even if reality shifts.
A forecast is an estimate, updated as new information comes in.
It changes constantly, tracking sales, costs, and market conditions in real time.
A forecast doesn't set a goal, it predicts what's likely to actually happen.
The difference is that a budget is a target, a forecast is a prediction.
This matters when a company decides whether to cut costs or invest more.
Together they show not just what a business wants, but what it expects.
