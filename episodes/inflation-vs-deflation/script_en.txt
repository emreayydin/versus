One makes your money worth less. The other makes it worth more.
But both can throw an economy off balance, just from opposite directions.
Inflation means prices rise, so each dollar buys a little less over time.
A coffee that cost three dollars might cost four dollars a few years later.
Mild inflation is normal, but high inflation erodes savings fast.
Deflation means prices fall, so each dollar buys more than before.
That sounds nice, but falling prices often mean people stop spending, waiting for lower prices.
Businesses earn less, wages get cut, and the economy can slow down further.
The difference is the direction: inflation shrinks the value of money, deflation grows it.
Central banks watch this balance closely, because too much of either can hurt growth.
Both are just two sides of the same question: what is money actually worth.
