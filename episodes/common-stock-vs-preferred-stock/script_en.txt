One kind of stock gets paid first. The other gets paid more, if things go well.
Both are called stock, but they play by different rules.
Common stock gives you a piece of the company and a vote at shareholder meetings.
Its dividend isn't fixed, it can grow, shrink, or vanish depending on profits.
If the company does well, common stock usually captures most of that growth.
Preferred stock pays a fixed dividend, agreed on in advance.
That dividend gets paid before common shareholders see any money.
In exchange, preferred shares usually come without a vote and with limited upside.
The difference is what you trade: preferred swaps upside for a fixed, earlier payment.
In a bad year, preferred holders can still get paid while common holders may get nothing.
Same word, stock, but two different promises about who gets paid, and when.
