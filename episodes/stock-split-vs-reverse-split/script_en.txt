A share price drops from one thousand to one hundred overnight, and nobody lost a cent.
That is a stock split. Its mirror image tells a different story.
In a stock split, each share is divided into several cheaper shares.
In a ten for one split, one share at one thousand becomes ten shares at one hundred.
A reverse split combines several shares into one more expensive share.
Ten shares at fifty cents become one share at five dollars.
The difference: a split makes more, cheaper shares, a reverse split makes fewer, pricier ones.
In both cases, the total value you own stays the same at that moment.
It matters as a signal: reverse splits often come from companies whose price has fallen very low.
A split cuts the pizza into more slices. A reverse split glues slices back together.
