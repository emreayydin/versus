Why can the unemployment rate fall while fewer people are actually working?
Two numbers claim to measure the same job market, but they count very different people.
The unemployment rate only counts people without a job who are actively searching for one.
The moment someone stops looking for work, this rate simply forgets they exist.
The labor force participation rate counts everyone who either works or wants to.
It shows how many working age people are engaged with the job market at all.
The difference is that one rate erases people who gave up, the other keeps counting them.
This is why unemployment can drop for the wrong reason, simply because people stopped trying.
Together, the two numbers show not just who has a job, but who is even trying to get one.
