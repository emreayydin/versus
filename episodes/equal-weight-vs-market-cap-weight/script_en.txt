In some indexes, a handful of giant companies decide most of the result.
Whether that happens depends on how the index is weighted.
Market-cap weight means each company counts by its total market value.
The biggest companies have the biggest influence on the index.
Equal weight gives every company the same share, regardless of size.
In an equal weight version of the S&P 500, each company starts at about zero point two percent.
The difference: market-cap weight follows the giants, equal weight spreads influence evenly.
Equal weight needs regular rebalancing, which can mean more trading costs.
It matters in years when a few large companies drive most of the gains.
Market-cap weight is ruled by size. Equal weight gives everyone one vote.
