A great return can come from skill or from borrowing. Two ratios tell you.
Two ratios separate the two.
ROE, return on equity, is profit divided by the money owners have put into the company.
ROA, return on assets, is profit divided by everything the company owns.
Picture one hundred dollars of assets, twenty from owners and eighty borrowed, earning ten dollars.
That is a ten percent ROA, but a fifty percent ROE.
The difference: ROA measures how well all assets earn, ROE how well the owners' money earns.
Debt lifts ROE without the business getting any better, so a high ROE can hide risk.
It matters when comparing banks or companies with very different amounts of debt.
ROA shows the engine. ROE shows the engine plus the borrowed fuel.
