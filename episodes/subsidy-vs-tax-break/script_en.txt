One shows up in the budget. The other never does.
Both change what something costs you, but the money moves in very different ways.
A subsidy is money the government pays out directly, often for a specific product.
It shows up as spending in the government's budget, so it's easy to count and to cut.
A tax break lets someone pay less tax than they would otherwise owe.
No money changes hands, so it counts as revenue the government never collects.
The difference is a subsidy is money paid out, a tax break is money never taken.
This matters because subsidies get counted and cut, but tax breaks often outlast them.
Same effect on your wallet, but a very different footprint in the government's books.
