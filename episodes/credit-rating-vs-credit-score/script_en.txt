Companies get letter grades. You get a number. Both ask the same thing.
Will the borrower pay back?
A credit rating is a grade like AAA or BB, given to governments, companies and their bonds.
Agencies such as S&P, Moody's and Fitch assign them.
A credit score is a number for an individual, in the US often between three hundred and eight hundred fifty.
It comes from your credit report: payments, balances and history.
The difference: a credit rating grades organizations and their debt, a credit score grades a person.
A lower grade or score usually means paying higher interest.
It matters because both quietly set the price of borrowing.
A rating is a report card for borrowers like Apple or Italy. A score is yours.
