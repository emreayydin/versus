One is what you pay. The other is what you get.
Understanding this split explains why cheap stocks aren't always a bargain.
Price is the number flashing on your screen, set by the last trade made.
It moves every second, pushed by supply, demand, and pure mood.
A stock can fall ten percent in a day even though nothing about the business changed.
Value is what a business is actually worth, based on its earnings and assets.
It shifts slowly, often over years, as the company grows or shrinks.
Two analysts can study the same numbers and still land on different values.
The difference is that price comes from the market, value comes from analysis.
That gap is why some investors look for stocks priced below their estimated worth.
Price tells you what others think today. Value asks what it's worth over time.
