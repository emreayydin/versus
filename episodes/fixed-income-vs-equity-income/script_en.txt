One pays you the same number every time. The other never promises a number at all.
Both are called income, but they come from completely different places.
Fixed income means lending money to a company or government.
In return you get a fixed interest payment, paid on a schedule.
That payment stays the same whether the borrower has a great year or a bad one.
Equity income means owning a small piece of a company through its shares.
If the company earns profit, it may pay you a dividend out of that profit.
That dividend can grow, shrink, or disappear, depending on how the business performs.
The difference is who carries the risk when things go wrong.
That's why bond income is called fixed, and dividend income is called variable.
Both are called income, but only one of them promises you a fixed number.
