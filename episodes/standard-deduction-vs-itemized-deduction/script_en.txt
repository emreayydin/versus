Every US taxpayer gets to make one choice that can lower the bill. Many never check it.
You can take the standard deduction or itemize, but not both.
The standard deduction is a fixed amount set each year that depends on your filing status.
No receipts needed: you subtract it from your income and you are done.
Itemizing means adding up specific expenses, like mortgage interest, state taxes and donations.
Say the standard amount is fifteen thousand and your itemized list adds up to eleven thousand.
Then the standard deduction lowers your taxable income more, so it is the better pick.
The difference: the standard deduction is a set amount, itemized deductions are the sum of your real costs.
Most filers take the standard one. Homeowners with big mortgages are more likely to itemize.
One is simple. The other only pays off when your list is longer than the fixed amount.
