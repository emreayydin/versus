A seventy-two month loan has a lower payment and can cost thousands more.
The length of a loan changes two numbers in opposite ways.
A short loan, like thirty-six months, has higher monthly payments.
But you pay interest for less time, so the total cost is lower.
A long loan, like seventy-two months, spreads the payments out and makes each one smaller.
Interest keeps running for twice as long, and longer loans often carry higher rates too.
The difference: a short loan costs more per month and less overall, a long loan the opposite.
With a long car loan, you can owe more than the car is worth for years.
It matters to compare the total paid, not just the monthly number.
A short loan hurts now and saves later. A long loan feels easy and costs more.
