Two annuities: one promises a rate, the other follows the market.
That changes what you can count on.
A fixed annuity pays a guaranteed interest rate set by the insurance company.
Your income is predictable, but it may not keep up with inflation.
A variable annuity puts your money into funds, so payouts rise and fall with markets.
They often carry higher fees, including insurance charges and fund costs.
The difference: a fixed annuity guarantees the rate, a variable annuity passes market risk to you.
Many annuities also charge surrender fees if you take money out early.
It matters to read the fee table, because it decides much of the result.
A fixed annuity is a promise. A variable annuity is a market ride inside an insurance wrapper.
