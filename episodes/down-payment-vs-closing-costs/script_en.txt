Buying a home means two separate piles of cash on day one. Most people only plan for one.
Here is what each pile pays for.
The down payment is the part of the price you pay yourself, so the loan is smaller.
On a three hundred thousand dollar home, twenty percent down is sixty thousand dollars.
Closing costs are the fees to finish the deal: lender fees, title, appraisal and taxes.
They often add another two to five percent of the price.
The difference: the down payment buys part of the home, closing costs pay for the paperwork.
Only the down payment becomes equity, meaning part of the house you actually own.
It matters when you save, because many buyers forget the second bill.
One payment is yours to keep in the house. The other is the cost of getting there.
