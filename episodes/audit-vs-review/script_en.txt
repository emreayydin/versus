One accountant checks everything. The other mostly just asks questions.
Both end with a report, but what stands behind it is very different.
An audit is the deepest look at a company's financial statements.
The accountant tests real transactions and confirms bank balances directly.
It ends with a formal opinion on whether the numbers are fairly stated.
A review is lighter and faster than an audit.
The accountant reads the numbers and asks management about anything unusual.
It compares figures to expectations, but never verifies the evidence itself.
The difference is the level of assurance: an audit verifies, a review only asks.
Banks and investors often require a full audit before approving large loans.
Same financial statements, but one certifies them, the other just glances them over.
