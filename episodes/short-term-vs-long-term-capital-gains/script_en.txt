Two people make the same profit on a stock. One pays far more tax, only because of timing.
In the US, a single date decides it.
A short-term capital gain comes from selling something you held for one year or less.
It is taxed like ordinary income, at your normal tax rate.
A long-term gain comes from holding for more than one year.
Those gains get lower tax rates, zero, fifteen or twenty percent at the federal level.
The difference: short-term gains are taxed like wages, long-term gains get a lower special rate.
Selling a few days too early can move a gain into the higher bracket.
It matters because rules and rates differ by country and can change.
Short-term is taxed like a paycheck. Long-term is rewarded for patience.
