A raise into a higher tax bracket does not shrink your paycheck. Here is why.
Two tax rates hide in every tax bill, and people mix them up all the time.
Your marginal tax rate is the rate on your last, highest dollar of income.
Your effective tax rate is your total tax divided by your total income.
Say the first forty thousand dollars is taxed at ten percent and the rest at twenty.
On sixty thousand dollars that is four thousand plus four thousand, so eight thousand in tax.
Your marginal rate is twenty percent, but your effective rate is only about thirteen.
The difference: the marginal rate applies to the next dollar, the effective rate to all of them on average.
Only the dollars above a bracket line get the higher rate, never the ones below it.
The marginal rate tells you what a raise costs. The effective rate tells you what you really pay.
