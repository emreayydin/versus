One loan can charge more in fees than you borrowed. The other rarely does.
Here's what actually separates a payday loan from a personal loan.
A payday loan is due in full on your very next payday, usually within two weeks.
Lenders often charge a flat fee, like fifteen dollars for every hundred borrowed.
Stretched over a year, that fee can equal an annual rate near four hundred percent.
A personal loan comes from a bank or credit union, usually repaid over months or years.
The lender checks your credit and sets an interest rate based on that history.
Typical rates run from about six to thirty percent a year, paid off in fixed installments.
The real difference is the interest rate and the time you're given to repay it.
That gap can turn a two hundred dollar shortfall into thousands of dollars owed.
Both are loans, but only one is built to be paid off quickly, not renewed again and again.
