People often spend more with a card than with cash. A feeling explains it.
Here is how each one works on you.
With cash, you physically hand over money and see your wallet get thinner.
Researchers call this the pain of paying, and it makes people more careful.
A card makes paying fast and easy, and the money leaves later, out of sight.
Cards also bring protection against fraud and a record of every purchase.
The difference: cash makes spending feel real, a card makes spending feel smooth.
Lost cash is usually gone, while a stolen card can be blocked.
It matters for budgets, where the feeling of paying can be a useful brake.
Cash hurts a little. A card hardly hurts at all, and that is the point.
