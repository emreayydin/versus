One pays you for holding on. The other pays you for letting go.
Both come from owning a share, but they show up in very different ways.
A dividend is a piece of the company's profit, paid straight to you.
It usually lands in your account every few months, whether the stock rises or not.
A capital gain is different, it only exists once you sell.
If the share price went up since you bought it, that increase is your gain.
The difference is who decides the payout, the company or the market.
This matters for taxes too, dividends are often taxed the year you receive them.
Capital gains are usually taxed only when you actually sell and lock in the profit.
Two ways to earn from the same share, one steady, one dependent on timing.
