Two bonds from the same company. One can turn into shares, the other never will.
That one extra right changes the whole deal.
A straight bond is a plain loan: you get interest, then your money back at the end.
Lend one thousand dollars at five percent, and you get fifty dollars a year.
A convertible bond adds a right to swap the bond for a fixed number of the company's shares.
Because that right has value, convertibles usually pay a lower interest rate, maybe two percent.
If the shares rise a lot, converting can be worth far more than the bond.
The difference: a straight bond only pays interest, a convertible bond can become stock.
If the shares never rise, the convertible holder simply earned less interest.
A straight bond is a loan. A convertible is a loan with a door into the company.
