One insurance ends when the term is up. The other is built to never end.
The reason comes down to what each one is actually designed to do.
Term life insurance covers you for a set number of years, like twenty or thirty.
If you die within that term your family gets paid, if not, coverage simply ends.
Whole life insurance covers you for as long as you live, as long as you keep paying.
Part of every whole life payment builds up a cash value that grows over time.
For the same payout, term can cost thirty dollars a month, whole life three hundred.
The difference is term buys pure protection, whole life buys protection plus savings.
That distinction matters most when you weigh decades of coverage against its cost.
In the end, one insures a period of your life, the other insures all of it.
