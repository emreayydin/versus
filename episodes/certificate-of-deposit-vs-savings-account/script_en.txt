Both accounts pay you interest. Only one lets you take your money out any day.
That freedom is exactly what you trade for the rate.
A savings account lets you add and withdraw money whenever you need it.
Its interest rate is variable, so the bank can lower or raise it at any time.
A certificate of deposit, or CD, locks your money for a set term, like one year.
In return, the rate is fixed for that whole term.
Take the money out early, and you usually pay a penalty, often some months of interest.
The difference: a savings account gives you flexibility, a certificate of deposit gives you a fixed rate.
In the US, both are usually insured by the FDIC up to two hundred fifty thousand dollars per bank.
A savings account is open all day. A CD is a deal with a date on it.
