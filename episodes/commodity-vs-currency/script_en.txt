One of them you can eat, burn, or build with. The other only buys things.
The difference explains why one can run out, and the other can be printed.
A commodity is a raw physical good, like wheat, oil, or copper.
Its value comes from what people can actually do with it.
There is only so much of it, and producing more takes time and real cost.
A currency is a token that people agree to accept in trade, like the dollar.
It has no use on its own, you can't eat it or build with it.
Its value depends on trust in the issuer, and how much of it exists.
The difference is that a commodity's value comes from use, a currency's value comes from trust.
This distinction matters most when trust in a currency weakens and prices shift.
One is mined or grown, the other is issued and managed.
