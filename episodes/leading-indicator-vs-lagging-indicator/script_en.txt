One number predicts the future, the other only confirms it.
Economists sort them into leading and lagging indicators, each working differently.
A leading indicator moves before the economy does, like new building permits.
When permits rise, builders expect more demand for houses in the months ahead.
A lagging indicator moves only after the economy has already changed direction.
The unemployment rate is a classic example, reacting months after a downturn begins.
The difference is timing, leading indicators forecast while lagging ones confirm.
Investors watch leading indicators to prepare, and lagging ones to verify what happened.
Together they show where the economy has been and where it might be heading.
