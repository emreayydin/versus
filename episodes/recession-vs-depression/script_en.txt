Both mean the economy is shrinking. Only one of them breaks it.
One happens somewhat regularly. The other happens once or twice a century.
A recession is two straight quarters of the economy producing less.
Jobs get cut, spending slows, but banks and savings mostly hold.
Most economies recover within a year or two.
A depression is a recession that goes much deeper and lasts far longer.
Unemployment can climb above twenty percent, and banks themselves can fail.
Recovery can take a decade, not months.
The difference is how deep it cuts and how long it stays.
That difference decides whether policy uses small tools or massive ones.
Same direction, different scale, one word for a dip and one for a collapse.
