Cash is built to slowly lose value. Gold is built to hold it.
Central banks target a small yearly inflation, often around two percent.
That means a hundred dollars today buys a little less next year.
Gold pays no interest and no dividend, ever.
But an ounce of gold has bought roughly the same bread for centuries.
The real difference is what each one protects: cash protects today, gold protects decades.
That difference matters most when inflation runs high for years, not months.
Neither one grows your money on its own; both simply hold it differently.
