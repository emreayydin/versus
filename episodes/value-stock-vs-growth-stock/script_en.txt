Why does one investor pay ten times earnings, and another pays fifty?
The answer is two very different bets on the same company.
A value stock trades below what its earnings or assets seem to justify.
Its investors are betting the market got the price wrong.
A growth stock trades high because investors expect earnings to rise fast.
The price today already assumes years of future growth.
The difference is what you're paying for, today's numbers or tomorrow's story.
This shows up clearly when interest rates rise, growth stocks often fall harder.
Both are ways of pricing the same uncertainty, just from opposite ends.
