Your card bill shows two numbers. Paying the small one can quietly cost you.
Here is what each one really means.
The statement balance is everything you charged in that billing cycle that is still unpaid.
Pay it in full by the due date, and new purchases usually cost no interest at all.
The minimum payment is the smallest amount that keeps your account in good standing.
The rest stays on the card and starts charging interest.
On one thousand dollars at twenty four percent a year, that is about twenty dollars a month.
The difference: the statement balance clears the bill, the minimum payment only avoids a late fee.
Paying only the minimum can stretch a small purchase over years.
The minimum keeps you out of trouble. The full balance keeps you out of interest.
