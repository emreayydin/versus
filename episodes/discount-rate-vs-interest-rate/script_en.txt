Same percentage, opposite direction. One makes money bigger, one makes it smaller.
Here is how two rates that look alike are used.
An interest rate is what a borrower pays for using money, so today's money grows into the future.
Lend one hundred dollars at ten percent, and one hundred ten comes back in a year.
A discount rate runs the other way. It turns future money into what it is worth today.
At ten percent, one hundred ten dollars promised in a year is worth one hundred today.
The difference: an interest rate grows money forward, a discount rate shrinks future money back to today.
In central banking, the discount rate also names the rate the Fed charges banks for short loans.
Interest tells you what money becomes. Discounting tells you what a promise is worth now.
