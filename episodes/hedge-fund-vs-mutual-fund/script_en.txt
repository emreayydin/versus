One can bet against the market. The other legally can't.
A hedge fund pools money from wealthy investors and big institutions.
It can borrow money, bet against stocks, and trade almost anything.
Fees often run two percent of assets, plus twenty percent of any profit.
A mutual fund pools money from everyday investors, often in retirement accounts.
It mainly buys stocks and bonds, and must report its holdings publicly.
Fees are usually under one percent a year, with no cut of the profits.
The difference isn't just size, it's what each one is legally allowed to do.
That gap shows up in risk taken, fees charged, and who's even allowed to invest.
Both try to grow money, just under very different rules.
