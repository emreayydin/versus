Why can a company fail with plenty of assets, but survive with almost none?
It comes down to two different kinds of trouble a business can be in.
Liquidity is about whether a company has enough cash to cover this month's bills.
A company can be liquid even while carrying large long-term debt, as long as cash keeps moving.
Solvency asks a bigger question: is everything the company owns worth more than everything it owes.
A company with two hundred thousand in assets and three hundred thousand in liabilities is insolvent.
A solvent company can still run out of cash and miss a payment today.
The difference is timing: liquidity is about today's cash, solvency is about the whole balance sheet.
This is why some profitable, solvent companies still collapse - they simply run out of cash first.
One problem can be fixed with a loan, the other means the company owes more than it will ever be worth.
