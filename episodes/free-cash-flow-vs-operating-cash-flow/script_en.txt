Lots of cash from customers, little left over. Investment explains the gap.
Two cash flow numbers show where it goes.
Operating cash flow is the cash the normal business brings in during a period.
It starts from profit, then adjusts for things like unpaid bills and depreciation.
Free cash flow is operating cash flow minus spending on equipment and buildings.
Bring in one hundred million, spend seventy on new factories, and free cash flow is thirty.
The difference: operating cash flow is cash earned, free cash flow is what is left after investing.
Dividends, buybacks and debt repayment all come out of free cash flow.
It matters because heavy investment can look bad today and pay off later, or never.
Operating cash flow is the income. Free cash flow is the spare money.
