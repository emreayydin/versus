One kind of loss you could have dodged. One kind you never could.
The split between them decides how much spreading your money actually helps.
Picture one company you own getting hit by a scandal, its stock falls hard.
That risk belongs to that one company alone, this is called unsystematic risk.
Hold shares in twenty different companies, and one scandal barely dents your total.
Now imagine interest rates rise everywhere, and every stock in the market dips.
That risk hits every company at once, this is called systematic risk.
Owning a hundred companies instead of one does nothing to soften this kind.
The difference: one risk fades away when you diversify, the other travels with the whole market.
This is why spreading money across companies shields you from one risk, never both.
Two risks share the word, only one of them can be spread away.
