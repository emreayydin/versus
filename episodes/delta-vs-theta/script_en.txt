An option can lose money even when the stock does nothing. Two Greek letters explain why.
They measure two different forces on the price.
Delta shows how much an option's price moves when the stock moves one dollar.
A delta of zero point five means the option gains about fifty cents per dollar the stock rises.
Theta shows how much value an option loses each day just from time passing.
A theta of minus five cents means about five cents less value per day, all else equal.
The difference: delta measures sensitivity to the stock price, theta measures the cost of time.
Time decay usually speeds up as the expiration date gets close.
It matters because an option buyer needs the move to come soon enough.
Delta is about direction. Theta is the clock ticking against the buyer.
