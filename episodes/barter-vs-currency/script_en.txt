One needs two people to want the exact same thing. The other doesn't.
Here's what actually changed when money entered the picture.
In a barter system, you trade goods or services directly for other goods.
A farmer with wheat has to find someone who wants wheat and has what he needs.
That double coincidence of wants makes trade slow and unpredictable.
Currency solves that problem with a shared token everyone accepts.
A baker can take money for bread and later buy shoes with the same money.
The value isn't in the paper itself, it's in the trust that others will accept it too.
The difference is that barter trades goods directly, while currency lets value travel through time.
This is why economies with money can support trade between strangers across cities and countries.
Barter ties value to a moment, currency lets value move.
