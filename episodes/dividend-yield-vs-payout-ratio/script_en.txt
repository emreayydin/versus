A big dividend can be comfortable or stretched. Two numbers tell you which.
They measure very different things.
Dividend yield is the yearly dividend divided by the share price.
A one hundred dollar share paying four dollars a year has a four percent yield.
The payout ratio is the share of the company's profit that goes out as dividends.
Earning five dollars and paying four means a payout ratio of eighty percent.
The difference: yield shows what you receive for the price, payout ratio shows what the company can afford.
A very high payout ratio leaves little room if profits fall.
It matters because a high yield can also be a sign that the share price has dropped.
Yield is the size of the check. Payout ratio is how hard it is to keep writing it.
