Everyone assumes selling more abroad means winning, and buying more means losing.
The numbers don't work quite that simply.
A trade deficit happens when a country buys more from the world than it sells.
In one recent year, the U.S. imported about three trillion dollars and exported about two trillion.
That gap gets covered by foreign money flowing in to buy U.S. stocks, bonds, and property.
A trade surplus happens when a country sells more to the world than it buys back.
Germany and China have done this for years, piling up foreign assets instead of foreign goods.
The real difference is what ends up in a country's hands, goods or claims on foreign wealth.
This shapes how much a country owns abroad, and how much it owes when debts come due.
Neither number alone tells you whether an economy is strong or weak.
