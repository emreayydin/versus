One is run by the government you vote for. The other isn't.
Both try to steer the economy, but they pull very different levers.
Fiscal policy is spending and taxes, decided by parliament or congress.
When a government builds roads or cuts taxes, that is fiscal policy.
It can boost jobs, but it takes months of debate to change course.
Monetary policy is the interest rate and money supply, set by a central bank.
When a central bank raises interest rates, borrowing gets more expensive.
It moves faster, one meeting can change the rate for the whole economy.
The difference is who decides and how fast they can act.
That gap matters most during a crisis, when speed can decide the outcome.
Two tools, same economy, pulled by different hands.
