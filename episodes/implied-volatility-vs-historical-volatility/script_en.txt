One volatility number looks backward. The other is hidden inside option prices.
Traders watch both, and they rarely agree.
Historical volatility measures how much a price actually moved over a past period.
If a stock swung about one percent a day last month, that is already known.
Implied volatility is worked out from today's option prices.
Expensive options mean the market expects bigger moves ahead.
The difference: historical volatility is measured from the past, implied volatility is priced for the future.
Before big news, like earnings, implied volatility often climbs above historical.
The VIX, often called the fear index, is built from implied volatility on S and P five hundred options.
Historical volatility is the weather report. Implied volatility is the forecast.
