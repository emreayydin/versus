Selling a loser can cut your taxes. Buying it back too soon cancels that.
In the US, a thirty day rule sits between the two.
Tax-loss harvesting means selling an investment at a loss to offset gains elsewhere.
A three thousand dollar loss can cancel out three thousand in gains.
A wash sale happens when you buy the same or a nearly identical investment within thirty days.
Then the loss cannot be claimed for now; it gets added to the cost of the new shares.
The difference: harvesting uses a real loss for taxes, a wash sale undoes it by buying back too soon.
The rule applies before and after the sale, so sixty-one days in total matter.
It matters at year end, when many people sell losers.
Harvesting is collecting the loss. A wash sale is accidentally throwing it back.
