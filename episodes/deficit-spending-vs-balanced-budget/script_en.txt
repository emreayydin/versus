Why does one government borrow billions while another spends only what it has?
The answer sits in two very different ideas about what a budget should do.
Deficit spending means a government spends more money than it collects in taxes.
It covers that gap by borrowing, usually by selling bonds to investors.
This can boost the economy during a downturn, but it adds to the national debt.
A balanced budget means spending exactly matches what the government takes in.
There is no borrowing, so debt does not grow from that year's decisions.
But it also leaves less room to react quickly if the economy suddenly slows.
The difference is simple: one plan borrows from the future, the other does not.
This choice shapes how much interest a country pays and how flexible it stays in a crisis.
Neither approach is simply right or wrong, it depends on the situation a country is in.
