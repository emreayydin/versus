A deer runs into your car. Which part of your insurance pays? Most people guess wrong.
Two optional car coverages split the work between them.
Collision coverage pays to fix your car after it hits another car or an object.
Crash into a pole or another car, and collision steps in, minus your deductible.
Comprehensive coverage pays for damage that does not come from a collision.
Theft, hail, fire, floods and falling trees all belong here.
And hitting an animal like a deer usually counts as comprehensive, not collision.
The difference: collision covers crashes, comprehensive covers almost everything else that happens to the car.
Neither one pays for the other driver's car. That is liability coverage.
Collision is about what you hit. Comprehensive is about what hits you.
