When someone dies, one person gets the money and another may have to do all the work.
These two roles are often confused.
A beneficiary is a person who receives something, like money from an account, a policy or a will.
Accounts with a named beneficiary can often pass to them directly.
An executor is the person named in a will to manage the estate.
They gather assets, pay debts and taxes, and then distribute what remains.
The difference: a beneficiary receives, an executor administers.
The same person can be both, which is common in families.
It matters to keep beneficiary forms updated, because they can override a will for some accounts.
The beneficiary gets the gift. The executor delivers it.
