One fee hits when you have too little money. The other hits when you're too slow.
Both come from banks or lenders, but they punish two completely different things.
An overdraft fee appears when you spend more than what's in your checking account.
The bank covers the gap for you, then charges you for covering it.
A typical overdraft fee sits around thirty five dollars, no matter how small the gap.
A late fee appears when you miss the due date on a bill or a credit card payment.
It doesn't matter how much you owe, only that the payment came in late.
Late fees often run between twenty five and forty dollars, sometimes with added interest.
The difference is what gets punished: too little money, or too little time.
This matters because paying off a balance won't stop an overdraft, and vice versa.
Same word, fee, but two completely different triggers sitting behind it.
