A truck you buy stays on the books for years. A truck you rent vanishes this month.
One is called capital expenditure, the other operating expenditure, and the split runs deep.
Capital expenditure buys things that last, like machines, trucks or buildings.
It isn't counted as a cost all at once, it's spread out over years through depreciation.
A hundred thousand dollar machine lasting ten years adds ten thousand dollars of cost each year.
Operating expenditure pays for running the business day to day, like rent and salaries.
It's counted as a cost immediately, in the same month the money leaves.
A company paying five thousand dollars rent sees all five thousand dollars hit this month's profit.
The difference is timing: one cost spreads out, the other hits immediately.
This matters when investors judge how profitable a company looks in its first year.
Same cash leaving the company, just measured on two very different clocks.
