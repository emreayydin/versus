A stock drops fast overnight. One order gets you out, the other might leave you stuck.
Both are meant to limit losses.
A stop-loss order turns into a market order once the price hits your stop.
You get out, but possibly at a worse price if the stock gaps down.
A stop-limit order turns into a limit order at your stop, with a minimum price you accept.
If the price falls below that limit before filling, the order may not execute at all.
The difference: a stop-loss guarantees an exit, a stop-limit guarantees a minimum price.
Neither one protects against every situation in a fast market.
It matters most for stocks that can jump on news.
A stop-loss says get me out. A stop-limit says get me out, but not below this.
