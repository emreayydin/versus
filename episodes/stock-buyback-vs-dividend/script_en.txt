A company with extra cash can hand it to owners in two main ways.
They feel very different to a shareholder.
A dividend pays cash straight to every shareholder.
You see the money land in your account, and in many countries it is taxed then.
A stock buyback means the company buys its own shares on the market and retires them.
Fewer shares remain, so each one owns a bigger slice of the company.
The difference: a dividend hands out cash, a buyback raises your share of the company instead.
Buybacks only help if the company does not pay too much for its own shares.
It matters for taxes and for how you see the return, as cash or as a higher stake.
A dividend is money in hand. A buyback is a bigger slice of the pie.
