Two bonds both repay in ten years. Only one of them reacts hard when rates move.
The difference sits in one word: when you actually get your money back.
Maturity is the day a bond returns its full face value to you.
A ten-year bond matures in ten years, that date never changes.
Duration measures how much a bond's price moves when interest rates shift.
It counts in years too, but it weighs every payment you get before the end.
A ten-year bond with yearly coupons has a shorter duration than a ten-year zero-coupon bond.
The difference is that maturity is a date, duration is a measure of risk.
That gap matters when rates rise, the higher the duration, the harder the price falls.
So two bonds can share the same maturity and still carry very different risk.
