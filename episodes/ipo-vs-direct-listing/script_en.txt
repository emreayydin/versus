One creates new shares. The other just opens the door to existing ones.
The choice changes who gets paid first, and who carries the early risk.
In an IPO, a company creates new shares and sells them before trading begins.
Banks help set the price, then hand those shares to big investors first.
The money raised goes straight to the company, funding growth or debt.
In a direct listing, no new shares get created at all.
Existing shareholders, like founders and employees, simply start selling what they hold.
There's no fixed price beforehand, so the market sets it on day one.
The real difference is who raises money, and who decides the starting price.
This matters most for companies needing fresh cash versus those that don't.
Both put a company on the exchange, just through different doors.
