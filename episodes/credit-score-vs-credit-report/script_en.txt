One is a single number. The other is the whole file behind it.
People treat them as the same thing, but lenders read them very differently.
A credit report lists every loan, every credit card, and every payment you made.
It also records late payments, defaults, and how long each account has existed.
A credit score takes that entire file and compresses it into one number.
That number usually runs from three hundred to eight hundred fifty, showing risk at a glance.
The difference is that the report is the evidence, and the score is the verdict.
Lenders often check both, the score for speed and the report for the details behind it.
Together they describe the same history, just at different levels of detail.
