One of these ends with you owning it. The other ends with you giving it back.
Both let you use something valuable today, long before you could pay for it in full.
A loan means borrowing money to buy something outright, like a car or a house.
You own it immediately, but the lender holds a claim on it until it's paid off.
Once the loan is paid off, it's fully yours, no strings attached.
A lease means paying for the right to use something for a set period of time.
You never own it, you're essentially renting it month by month.
When the lease ends, you return the item, unless you choose to buy it.
The difference is what happens at the end, ownership versus giving it back.
This shows up clearly with cars, where leases can mean lower payments but zero equity.
A loan builds something you keep, a lease buys you flexibility without long-term commitment.
