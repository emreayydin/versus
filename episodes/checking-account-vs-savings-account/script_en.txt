One account is built for spending. The other is built for waiting.
Both hold your money, but they work in very different ways.
A checking account is for daily use, paying bills, groceries, and rent.
You can move money in and out as often as you like, with almost no limits.
But it usually pays little or no interest on the money sitting there.
A savings account is built to hold money you don't need right away.
It pays interest, so your balance slowly grows over time.
But banks often limit how many withdrawals you can make each month.
The difference is speed versus growth, one for spending, one for saving.
Understanding this helps you decide where your paycheck should land first.
Neither account is better, they simply do two different jobs with your money.
