One makes you a part owner. The other makes you a lender.
The difference decides who gets paid first, and who gets paid more.
A stock is a small slice of a company's ownership.
When the company grows, the value of your stocks can rise together with it.
But if the company struggles, that value can fall just as fast.
A bond is a loan you give to a company or a government.
With bonds, you get fixed interest payments over a set time.
When the loan ends, you get your original money back.
The difference is risk and order: stocks share the upside, bonds get paid first.
That order matters most when a company runs into trouble or goes bankrupt.
Stocks and bonds simply sit on opposite ends of that same risk.
