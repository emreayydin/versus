A thousand dollars today and a thousand in a year are not worth the same.
Two simple ideas explain why, and they are mirror images.
Future value asks: what will money today grow into, at a given interest rate?
A thousand dollars at five percent becomes one thousand fifty dollars after a year.
Present value asks the reverse: what is money in the future worth today?
At five percent, one thousand fifty dollars next year is worth a thousand today.
The difference: future value moves money forward in time, present value moves it back.
It matters for loans, pensions and investments, which all compare money at different times.
The higher the rate, the less a future payment is worth today.
Future value grows money forward. Present value discounts it back to today.
