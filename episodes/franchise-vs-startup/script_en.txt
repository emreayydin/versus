One sells you a proven system. The other sells you an unproven idea.
Both promise a way to be your own boss, but the paths look very different.
A franchise means paying to use another company's name and playbook.
You get training, suppliers, and a brand people already recognize.
In return, you pay upfront fees and a share of every sale, often for years.
A startup means building something new from scratch, with no map to follow.
You keep more of what you earn, but you also carry all of the risk alone.
Franchises fail less often, because someone already tested the model before you did.
The difference is who takes the risk: the franchise buys certainty, the startup buys freedom.
This matters most when you're deciding how much control you want, and how much safety net.
Neither path guarantees success, they just distribute the risk differently.
