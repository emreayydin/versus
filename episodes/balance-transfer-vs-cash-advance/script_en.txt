Both use your credit card. One can save interest, the other costs from day one.
And the card rules for them are very different.
A balance transfer moves debt from one card to another, often to a lower rate.
Some cards offer zero percent for a year or more, but charge a fee of about three to five percent.
A cash advance means taking out cash using your credit card.
It usually has a fee, a higher rate, and interest starts the same day, with no grace period.
The difference: a balance transfer moves existing debt, a cash advance creates new and expensive debt.
When the intro rate on a transfer ends, the normal rate applies to what is left.
A balance transfer reshuffles debt. A cash advance borrows cash at a premium.
