One writes a check from personal savings, the other from someone else's fund.
Both put money into a startup before it earns anything, but the source changes everything after.
An angel investor is usually a wealthy individual funding a company with their own cash.
They often say yes after a single conversation, because it is their own risk to take.
A venture capitalist works for a fund that pools money from many investors and institutions.
An angel might risk fifty thousand dollars, a venture capitalist often puts in five million.
Before that money moves, a committee reviews the deal for weeks or months.
The difference is whose money is at stake and how many people must approve it.
This shapes whether a founder gets a fast small check or a slower, larger one.
Different money, different pace, same goal of building a company from nothing.
