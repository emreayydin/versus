Prices rising is normal. Prices doubling every week is not.
Both describe money losing value, but only one does it slowly.
Inflation is prices climbing at a modest, steady pace, often a few percent a year.
A cup of coffee might cost three dollars today and three ten next year.
Wages, contracts and prices adjust slowly, keeping the economy roughly balanced.
Hyperinflation is prices spiraling upward so fast that money loses value by the hour.
In extreme cases, prices have doubled within days, not years.
People rush to spend cash immediately, before it buys even less tomorrow.
The difference is speed: inflation erodes value slowly, hyperinflation destroys it almost overnight.
That difference decides whether savings quietly shrink over years or vanish within weeks.
Both are inflation, just separated by how fast trust in money collapses.
