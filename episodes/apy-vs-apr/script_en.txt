Twelve percent on a loan can cost more than twelve percent. The label tells you why.
Two short words, and the gap between them is compounding.
APR, the annual percentage rate, is the yearly rate without counting interest on interest.
On loans and cards, it can also include some fees, so you can compare offers.
APY, the annual percentage yield, includes compounding, the interest earned on earlier interest.
Twelve percent APR charged monthly works out to about twelve point seven percent APY.
The difference: APR leaves compounding out, APY counts it in.
That is why banks often show APY on savings, where the bigger number looks better.
Comparing offers only works when both use the same measure.
APR is the rate on paper. APY is what the rate adds up to in a year.
