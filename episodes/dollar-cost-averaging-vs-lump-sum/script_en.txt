Twelve thousand dollars to invest. All of it today, or a thousand each month?
Both approaches have names, and the research on them is surprising.
Lump sum means investing the whole amount at once.
Dollar cost averaging means splitting it into equal amounts invested at regular intervals.
Because the amount is fixed, you automatically buy more shares when prices are low.
A Vanguard study found the lump sum came out ahead about two thirds of the time in the past.
That is mainly because markets rose more often than they fell, so money waiting on the side missed out.
The difference: lump sum takes all the timing risk at once, averaging spreads it out over time.
It matters for how a fall right after investing would feel, which is a personal question, not a formula.
Lump sum is one decision. Dollar cost averaging is many small ones in a row.
