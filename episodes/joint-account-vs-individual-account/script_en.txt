Couples often share an account. Legally, that means sharing much more than the balance.
Here is what changes.
An individual account belongs to one person, who alone controls the money.
A joint account has two or more owners, and each can usually deposit and withdraw everything.
Either owner could empty it without asking the other.
If one owner has debts, creditors may be able to reach the joint money too.
The difference: an individual account has one owner, a joint account gives each owner full access.
Many couples use both: a joint one for shared bills and separate ones for personal money.
It matters because the exact rules depend on the bank and the country.
Individual means mine. Joint means ours, with all the trust that requires.
