Two cards both promise you money back, but only one tells you what it's worth.
The words sound similar, but the two systems are built very differently.
Cashback gives you a fixed percent of what you spend, paid back as real money.
Spend a hundred dollars, get two dollars back, and that two dollars stays two dollars.
Rewards points give you units instead, and the bank decides what each unit is worth.
The same hundred dollars might earn two hundred points, worth one to three dollars.
That value can also shift overnight if the program changes its rules.
The difference is cashback has one fixed value, points have a value the bank can move.
That matters most when you're comparing two cards that look identical on paper.
One card pays you a number, the other pays you a promise you still have to translate.
