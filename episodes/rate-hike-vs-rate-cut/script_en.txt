One central bank announcement can change your mortgage, your savings and the stock market.
It usually goes in one of two directions.
A rate hike means the central bank raises its key interest rate.
Borrowing gets more expensive, so people and companies tend to spend and borrow less.
That is meant to cool down inflation.
A rate cut lowers the key rate, making loans cheaper to encourage spending.
The difference: a hike works like a brake on the economy, a cut works like pressing the gas.
Savers often earn more after hikes and less after cuts.
It matters with a delay, because the effects can take a year or more to show.
A hike slows things down. A cut speeds them up.
