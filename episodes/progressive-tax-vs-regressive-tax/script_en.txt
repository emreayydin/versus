One tax takes a bigger bite the more you earn. The other takes the same bite.
Both show up on your paycheck or your receipt, but they work in opposite ways.
A progressive tax raises its rate as income rises.
Someone earning more pays a higher percentage of that income in tax.
Income tax in many countries works this way, climbing through brackets.
A regressive tax charges the same rate no matter how much you earn.
A sales tax on groceries takes that rate whether your income is small or large.
For someone earning less, that flat rate eats up a bigger share of their income.
The difference is not the rate itself, it's how much of your income that rate represents.
This matters most for lower income households, where flat taxes take a larger bite.
Same word, tax, but two very different relationships between what you earn and pay.
