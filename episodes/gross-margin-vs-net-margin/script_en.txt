A company can show a strong profit margin and still be losing money.
Two numbers share the word margin, but they stop counting at different points.
Gross margin only subtracts what it costs to make or buy the product.
It says nothing about rent, salaries, marketing, or interest on debt.
Net margin subtracts every cost a business has, all the way down.
What survives that is the actual profit left for the owners.
Out of a hundred dollars in revenue, sixty might survive as gross margin, but only ten as net margin.
The difference is how many costs have already been subtracted before you see the number.
A retailer can have a thin gross margin but a healthy net margin if it runs lean everywhere else.
Gross margin shows what a product earns, net margin shows what the company actually keeps.
