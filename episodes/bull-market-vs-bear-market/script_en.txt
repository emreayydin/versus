Why do investors name entire markets after animals?
One tracks years of rising prices, the other years of falling ones.
A bull market means stock prices are climbing over an extended period.
It usually comes with rising confidence and more people buying stocks.
Bull markets can last for years, sometimes even a decade.
A bear market means prices have fallen twenty percent or more from a peak.
It often brings fear, selling, and shrinking savings.
The difference is direction: bulls push prices up, bears push them down.
Knowing which one you're in changes how you read your portfolio's swings.
Markets shift between the two endlessly, like weather changing seasons.
