One card asks for your money before it trusts you. The other trusts you first.
The difference between them decides who's really taking the risk.
A secured card asks you to deposit money first, often a few hundred dollars.
That deposit becomes your credit limit, and the bank holds it as security.
If you don't pay, the bank simply keeps your deposit.
An unsecured card gives you a credit limit without any deposit at all.
The bank bases that limit on your income and your credit history instead.
If you don't pay, the bank absorbs the loss and reports it to your credit file.
The difference is simple: who carries the risk if the bill goes unpaid.
That difference matters most when someone is building credit from scratch.
Same plastic, same swipe, but very different promises behind it.
