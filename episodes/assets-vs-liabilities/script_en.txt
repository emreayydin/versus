One puts money in your pocket. The other takes it out.
Most people mix up the two, especially when it comes to their own home.
An asset is anything that puts money into your pocket over time.
A rental property that pays more in rent than it costs is an asset.
So is a stock that pays dividends or a savings account that earns interest.
A liability is anything that takes money out of your pocket over time.
A car loan, a credit card balance, or a mortgage on a home you live in are liabilities.
Even a house can be a liability if the costs of owning it exceed what it earns you.
The real difference isn't what something looks like, it's which direction the money flows.
This distinction matters most when someone looks wealthy but actually owns very little.
Assets fill your pocket, liabilities empty it, no matter what the thing is called.
