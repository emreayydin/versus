Two funds both set money aside. Only one knows exactly what it's saving for.
One is built around a known date and number, the other around the unknown.
A sinking fund is money a company sets aside to pay off a debt on a fixed date.
Say a company owes a hundred thousand dollars in bonds due in five years.
It sets aside twenty thousand dollars a year, so the debt is covered without a shock.
A reserve fund is money kept aside for costs nobody can name in advance.
A building's reserve fund might cover a roof that fails or a pipe that bursts.
Nobody knows the date or the amount, so the fund just sits there, ready.
The difference is that a sinking fund pays for something certain, a reserve fund for something possible.
Investors check sinking funds before buying bonds, buyers check reserve funds before buying an apartment.
Both are saving ahead, one for a fact, the other for a chance.
