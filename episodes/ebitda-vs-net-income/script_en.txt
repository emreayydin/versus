One profit on the slide, a smaller one in the report. Both can be true.
They stop at different lines.
EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization.
It shows how much the core business earns before those costs.
Net income is what remains after every cost, including interest, taxes and depreciation.
A firm with fifty million EBITDA might keep only fifteen million as net income.
The difference: EBITDA ignores financing and wear on equipment, net income includes everything.
That is why heavily indebted companies like to talk about EBITDA.
It matters because interest and worn-out machines are real costs that still need cash.
EBITDA is the operating view. Net income is the bottom line.
