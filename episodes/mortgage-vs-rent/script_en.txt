Every month you pay to live somewhere, so why does only one payment come back to you?
By the end, you will see exactly where each payment actually goes.
With renting, you pay a landlord for the right to use the space.
The landlord keeps ownership, covers repairs, and can raise the rent later.
None of that rent payment ever turns into equity for you.
With a mortgage, you borrow money from a bank to buy the home outright.
Part of each payment covers interest, the rest slowly pays down what you owe.
Over the years, that shrinking loan turns into a growing share of ownership.
The real difference is that rent pays for using a home, a mortgage pays toward owning it.
That difference matters most when you plan to stay somewhere for many years.
One turns housing costs into flexibility, the other turns them into an asset.
