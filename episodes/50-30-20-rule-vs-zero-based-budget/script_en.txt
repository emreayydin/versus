Two popular budgets. One takes a minute to set up, the other plans every single dollar.
Which one fits depends on how much detail you want.
The 50/30/20 rule splits income into fifty percent needs, thirty percent wants and twenty percent savings.
It is simple and flexible, but rough.
A zero-based budget gives every dollar a job, until income minus plans equals zero.
Savings and debt payments are planned as categories too.
The difference: 50/30/20 sets three big buckets, zero-based plans each dollar individually.
Zero-based takes more time, but shows exactly where money goes.
It matters for people with irregular income, where flexibility can help.
One is a quick sketch. The other is a detailed map.
