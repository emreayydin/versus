You have health insurance and still get a bill. Two words usually explain it.
They describe different parts of what you pay yourself.
A deductible is the amount you pay for covered care before your insurance starts sharing the cost.
With a one thousand dollar deductible, the first thousand of covered bills is mostly yours.
A copay is a fixed fee for a specific service, like twenty five dollars for a doctor visit.
In many plans, copays apply to some services even before the deductible is reached.
The difference: the deductible is a yearly amount you pay first, a copay is a flat fee per visit.
It matters when you compare plans, because a low premium often comes with a high deductible.
The exact rules are in your plan documents, and they differ from plan to plan.
The deductible is your share up front. The copay is your share each time.
