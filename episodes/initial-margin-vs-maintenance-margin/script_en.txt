Borrowing to buy stock has two limits. The second one is where margin calls begin.
Both are called margin, but they work at different moments.
Initial margin is the share of the purchase you must pay with your own money.
In the US it is usually fifty percent: buy ten thousand dollars of stock, put up five thousand.
Maintenance margin is the minimum equity you must keep in the account afterwards.
The rules set it at twenty five percent at least, and many brokers ask for more.
With five thousand borrowed, a call comes if the stock falls below about six thousand seven hundred.
The difference: initial margin is the entry ticket, maintenance margin is the floor you must stay above.
Fall below the floor and you must add cash, or the broker can close the position for you.
Initial margin gets you in. Maintenance margin decides how long you can stay.
