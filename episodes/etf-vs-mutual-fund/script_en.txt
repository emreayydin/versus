One prices all day. The other prices only once, after the close.
Both are baskets of investments, but they get in and out in very different ways.
An ETF trades on an exchange, just like a share of a company.
Its price moves throughout the day as buyers and sellers meet.
You can buy or sell an ETF any time the market is open.
A mutual fund also pools money from many investors into one fund.
But it only calculates one price per day, after the market closes.
Orders placed during the day all get that same closing price.
The difference is timing: an ETF prices by the minute, a mutual fund once a day.
This matters most if you care about the exact price you pay or receive.
Both can hold the same stocks - the difference is simply how you get in and out.
