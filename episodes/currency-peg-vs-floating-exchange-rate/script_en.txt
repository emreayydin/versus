Some currencies barely move against the dollar for years. Others change every second.
Governments choose between two systems.
A currency peg fixes the exchange rate to another currency, like the dollar.
The central bank buys and sells reserves to keep the rate in place.
A floating exchange rate is set by supply and demand in currency markets.
It can move a lot with interest rates, trade and investor mood.
The difference: a peg holds the rate steady by policy, a float lets the market set it.
A peg can break if the central bank runs out of reserves to defend it.
It matters for trade, travel and anyone who borrowed in a foreign currency.
A peg is an anchor. A float is a sail in the wind.
