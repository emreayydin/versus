Unpaid debt can follow you in two very different ways. One reaches your paycheck.
The other attaches itself to what you own.
A garnishment is a court order that takes part of your wages or bank account to pay a debt.
Your employer then sends part of each paycheck directly to the creditor.
A lien is a legal claim on a piece of property, like a house or a car, as security for a debt.
You can usually keep using the property, but selling it means the debt gets paid first.
The difference: a garnishment takes money as it comes in, a lien blocks the value of what you own.
Some liens are normal, like the one a bank holds on a car until the loan is paid.
The exact rules and limits depend on where you live and the type of debt.
Garnishment takes from your income. A lien holds on to your property.
