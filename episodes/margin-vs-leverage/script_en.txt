One word is the deposit you post. The other is what that deposit lets you do.
By the end, you will see how one number creates the other.
Margin is the cash your broker asks you to put up before you trade.
To control ten thousand dollars of stock, a broker might ask for two thousand as margin.
That two thousand sits as collateral, a kind of deposit against the trade.
Leverage is the ratio between what you control and what you actually own.
Ten thousand controlled with two thousand of your own means five to one leverage.
The difference is margin is the deposit, leverage is the multiple that deposit buys.
This matters most when prices move, because leverage multiplies both gains and losses.
Same trade, two numbers: one shows what you paid, the other shows how far it can swing.
