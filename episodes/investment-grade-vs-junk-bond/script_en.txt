One company pays four percent to borrow, another ten. A grade explains it.
Rating agencies sort bonds into two big groups.
Investment grade bonds have ratings of BBB minus or higher.
The agencies see a lower chance that the borrower fails to pay.
Junk bonds, also called high yield, sit below that line.
Because default is more likely, they must offer much higher interest.
The difference: investment grade means lower default risk, junk means higher risk and higher interest.
Many funds are only allowed to hold investment grade bonds.
It matters when a company is downgraded, because forced selling can follow.
Investment grade is the safer borrower. Junk is the riskier one paying for your nerves.
