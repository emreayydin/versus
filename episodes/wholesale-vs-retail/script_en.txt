Why does the exact same shirt cost eight dollars in one deal and thirty in another?
The gap comes down to who's buying, and how many units at once.
Wholesale means selling large quantities straight to businesses, not to individual shoppers.
A wholesaler sells one shirt for eight dollars, a store then sells it for thirty.
Retail means selling single items directly to the person who will actually use them.
The store covers rent, staff wages, and unsold stock out of that higher price.
The difference is what the price actually pays for: bulk efficiency or individual service.
This gap is why a wholesale market and a shopping mall can price the same item so differently.
Same product, same factory, but two completely different economics get it to your hand.
