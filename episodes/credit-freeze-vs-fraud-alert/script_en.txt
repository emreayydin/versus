Someone stole your data. Two free tools protect your credit, and they are not equal.
Both cost nothing in the US. One just works much harder.
A fraud alert tells lenders to verify your identity before opening new credit in your name.
You contact one credit bureau, it tells the other two, and the alert lasts one year.
A credit freeze blocks most new lenders from seeing your credit report at all.
Without your report, most lenders will not approve a new account, so a thief gets stuck.
You set it at each of the three bureaus, and it stays until you lift it.
The difference: a fraud alert asks lenders to be careful, a credit freeze locks the door.
You have to lift the freeze yourself before applying for a new loan or card.
A fraud alert is a warning sign. A credit freeze is a lock.
