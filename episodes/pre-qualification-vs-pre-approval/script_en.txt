Two home buyers both say they are approved. Only one has been checked.
Lenders use two very similar words for two very different steps.
Pre-qualification is a quick estimate based on what you tell the lender about yourself.
It often uses only a soft credit check, and nothing you say is verified yet.
Pre-approval means the lender checks your documents, like pay slips, bank statements and credit.
You usually get a letter that says how much you may borrow, under conditions.
The difference: pre-qualification is based on your word, pre-approval is based on your papers.
Sellers often take an offer more seriously when a pre-approval letter comes with it.
Neither one is a final loan. The home and your finances still get checked again.
Pre-qualification tells you what might work. Pre-approval shows you did the homework.
