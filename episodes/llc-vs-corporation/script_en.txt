Why do some businesses get taxed twice, and others only once?
It comes down to two structures, and one small word: double.
An LLC stands for limited liability company, owned by members.
Profits pass straight through to the owners, taxed only on their personal return.
Paperwork stays light, and owners can run it almost like a partnership.
A corporation is owned by shareholders, and run by a board.
The company itself pays tax on profits, then shareholders pay tax again on dividends.
That structure makes it easier to raise money by selling shares to investors.
The difference is who gets taxed, and how easily ownership can be split and sold.
This matters most when a business plans to raise outside investment or go public.
Same goal, running a business, but two very different legal skeletons underneath.
