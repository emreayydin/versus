One number resets every year. The other one never does.
Both come from the same government, but they answer very different questions.
A budget deficit is what happens when spending is higher than income in one year.
Say a government spends two trillion dollars but only collects one trillion in taxes.
National debt is the sum of every yearly deficit added together, minus any surpluses.
Over decades, those yearly gaps stack up into one running total measured in trillions.
The difference is that a deficit is one year's gap, while debt is everything added up.
This matters because a shrinking deficit can still leave the total debt growing.
So watching only one number tells you half the story, not the whole one.
