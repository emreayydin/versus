Both pay you every month for life. Only one of them you had to buy.
One comes from your job, the other you can build on your own.
A pension is a promise your employer makes while you work.
Each year of work adds a bit more to the monthly payment you'll get later.
The employer, sometimes with the state, invests money to keep that promise.
An annuity is a contract you buy from an insurance company.
You hand over a lump sum now, and they pay you a fixed amount later.
The size of that payment depends on your age, your money, and the deal you signed.
The difference is a pension is earned through work, an annuity is bought with savings.
This matters most when you change jobs, or want to turn savings into income.
One rewards years worked, the other turns savings into steady payments.
