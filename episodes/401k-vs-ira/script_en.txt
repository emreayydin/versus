One account only exists because you have a job. The other you can open on your own.
Both grow tax-advantaged, but the rules that shape them are very different.
A 401k is set up through your employer, often with matching contributions.
Its yearly contribution limit sits close to twenty three thousand dollars.
An IRA you open yourself, at a bank or a brokerage, no employer required.
Its limit is lower, around seven thousand dollars, and you choose where the money sits.
The difference is who opens the account and who decides where the money goes.
That matters when you change jobs, an IRA stays with you, a 401k often does not.
Two different doors, both leading to the same tax-advantaged retirement savings.
