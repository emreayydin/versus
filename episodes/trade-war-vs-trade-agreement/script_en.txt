A trade agreement is a deal between countries to lower or remove tariffs on each other's goods.
If a ten percent tariff disappears, a car priced at eleven thousand drops to ten thousand.
Companies on both sides can sell more, because their goods just got cheaper for foreign buyers.
A trade war is the opposite: one country raises tariffs, and the other raises tariffs back.
A twenty percent tariff can turn a thousand dollar shipment into twelve hundred dollars.
Prices for those goods rise, and both countries end up selling less to each other.
The difference is simple: one direction lowers tariffs, the other raises them.
This matters for anyone buying imported goods, since tariffs from either side show up in the price.
One direction opens markets between countries, the other closes them.
