Two five-year bonds. One pays more because it is more likely to disappoint.
The difference is who borrowed your money.
A Treasury bond is a loan to the US government.
Because the government can tax and print its own currency, default risk is seen as very low.
A corporate bond is a loan to a company, which can run into trouble or even go bankrupt.
To make up for that risk, companies usually pay a higher interest rate.
The difference: Treasuries are backed by the government, corporate bonds only by one company.
The extra interest a company pays is called the credit spread.
It matters in a crisis, when credit spreads can suddenly widen.
Treasuries pay for safety. Corporate bonds pay you to carry more risk.
