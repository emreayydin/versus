You can lose money because someone does not pay, or because nobody wants to buy.
Those are two separate risks with two names.
Credit risk is the chance that a borrower does not pay back what they owe.
A company goes bankrupt, and its bonds pay back only part, or nothing.
Liquidity risk is the chance you cannot sell something quickly without cutting the price.
A house can be worth three hundred thousand, but selling it this week may mean taking far less.
The difference: credit risk is about not being paid, liquidity risk is about not finding a buyer in time.
In a crisis, the two often show up together.
Credit risk asks if you get paid. Liquidity risk asks how fast you can get out.
