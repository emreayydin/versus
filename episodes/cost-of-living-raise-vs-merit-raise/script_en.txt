You got a three percent raise. Prices rose three percent. In real terms, nothing changed.
That is why the type of raise matters.
A cost-of-living raise adjusts pay so it keeps up with rising prices.
It is often given to everyone, regardless of performance.
A merit raise rewards an individual for strong work or new skills.
It can move your pay ahead of inflation, not just level with it.
The difference: a cost-of-living raise protects buying power, a merit raise increases it.
Some companies combine both into one number, which hides the split.
It matters when you compare your raise with the inflation rate.
One raise helps you stand still. The other helps you move forward.
