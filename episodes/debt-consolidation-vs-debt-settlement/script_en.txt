One pays off everything you owe. The other convinces lenders to accept less.
Both are ways to deal with debt you can't pay as agreed, but they work very differently.
Debt consolidation combines several debts into one new loan, usually at a lower rate.
You still repay the full amount you owed, just in a single monthly payment.
A bank or lender approves this loan based on your credit history.
Debt settlement means negotiating with creditors to accept less than you owe.
This usually happens after missed payments, often through a settlement company.
The forgiven amount can count as taxable income, and your credit score often drops.
The difference is that consolidation repays every dollar, while settlement reduces what's owed.
This distinction matters most when deciding whether to protect your credit or reduce your balance.
Either way, the debt existed first, and how it gets repaid is what changes.
