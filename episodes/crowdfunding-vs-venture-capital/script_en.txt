Why do some startups ask millions of strangers, and others ask just one investor?
Both raise money for an idea, but what happens after looks nothing alike.
Crowdfunding lets anyone post an idea online and ask thousands of strangers for money.
Backers might get the product early, a small reward, or occasionally a tiny slice of equity.
Each backer usually puts in only a small amount, spread across thousands of people.
Venture capital comes from professional investors who manage large funds of money.
They write large checks in exchange for a real share of ownership in the company.
In return, they often take a board seat and expect a say in major decisions.
The real difference is control: crowdfunding gives you cash, venture capital gives you a partner.
That distinction matters most when a company needs guidance, not just money, to survive.
Both can fund the same dream, they simply ask for different things in return.
