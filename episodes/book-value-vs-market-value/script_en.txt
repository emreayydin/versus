A company can be worth two totally different numbers at the same time.
One is written down. The other is decided by buyers and sellers.
Book value adds up everything a company owns, then subtracts what it owes.
It comes straight from the balance sheet, calculated once a quarter.
Market value is what buyers actually pay for the whole company, right now.
Multiply the share price by the number of shares, and that is market value.
A company might show a book value of five hundred million and a market value of eight hundred million.
The difference is that book value looks backward, while market value looks forward.
When the two diverge sharply, investors start asking what the market sees that the books don't.
One records the past cost, the other reflects today's judgment.
