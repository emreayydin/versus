A country and a company can both fail to repay. What happens next is completely different.
That is the core of this comparison.
Sovereign debt is money borrowed by a national government, usually through bonds.
A country cannot be shut down like a company, so defaults end in negotiations.
Corporate debt is money borrowed by a company.
If it fails, bankruptcy courts can sell assets to repay lenders in a set order.
The difference: sovereign debt rests on a state's taxing power, corporate debt on one company.
Governments borrowing in their own currency face different risks than those borrowing in dollars.
It matters for bond prices, because trust in the borrower drives the interest rate.
A country negotiates its debts. A company can be broken up for them.
