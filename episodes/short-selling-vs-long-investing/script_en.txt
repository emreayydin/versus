One makes money when a price falls. The other needs it to rise.
Both are ways of betting on a stock's price, but they work in opposite directions.
Long investing means buying a share and simply holding it.
If the price goes up, the value of what you own goes up with it.
Short selling means borrowing a share, selling it, and buying it back later.
If the price falls, you buy it back cheaper and keep the difference as profit.
But if the price rises instead, the loss for a short seller has no upper limit.
The difference is how much you can lose if the price moves against you.
This matters most when someone underestimates how far a price can move.
Both are tools for reading a price. They just read it in opposite directions.
