One number comes from a law. The other comes from a budget.
Both try to answer what work should be worth, but they start from opposite places.
The minimum wage is a legal floor set by government, the same across a region.
It can stay unchanged for years, even as rent and food keep getting more expensive.
The living wage is calculated from real costs: rent, food, transport, in one place.
It moves whenever prices move, and it differs from city to city.
The difference: one is a legal minimum, the other is an estimate of survival costs.
In expensive cities, the legal minimum can sit far below any living wage estimate.
Neither number says what someone should earn, only what each measure is counting.
