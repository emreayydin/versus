One protects your money. The other asks it to take a risk.
By the end, you'll see exactly where that risk goes, and where it doesn't.
Saving means putting money into an account that pays a small interest rate.
The balance moves up only slightly, but it never suddenly drops.
In many countries, a bank guarantee protects that money even if the bank fails.
Investing means buying something like a stock or a bond and hoping it grows in value.
That value can climb quickly over the years, but it can also fall just as fast.
There is no guarantee, and no insurance if the price drops.
The difference is simple: saving protects the amount, investing risks it for growth.
After ten years, a small savings return can look very different from stock market growth.
Saving keeps your money steady, investing lets it grow, and each does a different job.
