One keeps your money safe. The other puts it at risk to grow.
Both start from the same coin, but send it down very different paths.
Saving means putting money into an account that barely changes in value.
The bank pays you a small interest rate, often below inflation.
Your balance stays steady, but its buying power can quietly shrink.
Investing means using that money to buy part of a company or a bond.
Its value can move up and down with markets, sometimes sharply.
Over many years, it has historically grown faster than typical savings accounts.
The difference is what you trade for safety: certainty now, growth later.
This matters most when you decide how soon you might need the money.
Saving keeps money ready, investing lets time work on it.
