Two funds earn the same return. One ratio calls them equal, the other does not.
Both ratios ask the same question: how much return did you get for the risk?
The Sharpe ratio divides the extra return by how much the returns swing, up and down.
So a fund that jumps up a lot gets punished just like one that drops a lot.
The Sortino ratio only counts the swings below a target, the ones that actually hurt.
Big jumps up are not treated as risk at all.
The difference: Sharpe counts all volatility as risk, Sortino only counts the downside.
A fund with rare drops and big rallies looks better under Sortino than under Sharpe.
Neither ratio predicts the future. Both only describe what already happened.
Sharpe asks how bumpy the ride was. Sortino asks how often it went downhill.
