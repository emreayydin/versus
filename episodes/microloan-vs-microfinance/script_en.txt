Most people use these two words as if they mean the same thing.
One is a single loan. The other is the system that makes loans like that possible.
A microloan is a small loan, often under a few hundred dollars, with no collateral required.
Someone borrows two hundred dollars to buy a sewing machine for their tailoring business.
They repay it in small weekly amounts over about ten months.
Microfinance is the wider system that offers this kind of loan in the first place.
It also includes savings accounts, small insurance, and ways to send and receive payments.
These services are built for people banks usually turn away, often without any credit history.
The difference is scale: a microloan is one transaction, microfinance is the whole structure around it.
This matters because a single microloan can fail while the wider system still helps thousands.
So a microloan is one piece. Microfinance is the puzzle it belongs to.
