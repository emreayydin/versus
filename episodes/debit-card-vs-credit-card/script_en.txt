One spends money you have. The other spends money you don't - yet.
Both look the same at checkout, but the money moves in completely different ways.
A debit card pulls money straight out of your bank account, right away.
If the account is empty, the payment simply fails.
A credit card pays the store for you, and the bank lends you that amount.
You owe the bank that money, and you pay it back later, usually once a month.
Wait past that date, and the bank charges interest on what you still owe.
The difference is timing: debit moves your money now, credit moves the bank's money first.
That timing matters when you're short on cash, or when you want to build a credit history.
Neither one is better - they just move money on a different clock.
