One spends money you have. The other spends money someone lent you.
The plastic looks the same, but what happens behind it is not.
A debit card is wired straight to your bank account.
Every payment pulls money out of that account right away.
There's no borrowing involved, so there's nothing to pay back later.
A credit card pays on your behalf, and the bank tracks what you owe.
That balance falls due later, and unpaid parts start collecting interest.
This borrowed spending is also what builds a credit history over time.
The difference is timing: one moves your money now, the other moves it later.
That timing matters most when a bill lands right before payday.
Same size, same slot in your wallet, but a completely different promise.
